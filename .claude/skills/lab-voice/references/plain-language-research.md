# Plain-language health writing: what official sources say

This is the evidence behind the "Say it once" and "Long pages" sections of `../SKILL.md`. It records what U.S. government guidance on plain language and health literacy says about structure, length, repetition and numbers. The aim is for the lab's rules to rest on sources rather than taste.

**How it was gathered.** Pages were read on 2026-10-11 with a fetch tool that returns a model's extraction of the page, not the raw page. Each "Claim (our words)" cell says no more than its quote; anything wider is marked "Our inference". Quotes are short and attributed, each under 25 words. "Date shown" is the date the page showed, or "not shown". The "Check" column says whether a script later found the quote on the raw page; see "Quote verification" at the end.

**What PEP is for.** Both papers describe training for clinical conversation: trust, open dialogue and shared goals.

**What the lab takes from it.** Our inference, not a PEP finding; no source here tests it for writing:

- Partnership language: "you and your care team decide", not "patients must".
- Acknowledge the reader's own situation and priorities before giving information.
- Offer information as something to discuss with the care team, not as orders.
- No judgment about adherence, missed treatments or choices.
- Curiosity over certainty: say what is unknown.

**What does not carry over.** Asking open questions and listening need a conversation, which a page cannot have. Role play, team building and the follow-up session do not apply to writing. The pilot's survey ratings say nothing about how readers understand a page.

**Foundation pages.** A site-limited search of cff.org returned no page describing PEP. It returned an application PDF on a `/media/.../download` path, which returned `403` to scripts in earlier lab tests, and a personal author page. Neither was fetched, so no Foundation page is cited here.

## What this does not tell us

- **Strength of evidence.** Most of this is guidance and rubric items. The pages say the literature supports them, but we did not read it. None of it shows that a rule improves understanding for our readers.
- **Audience.** The federal pages (Digital.gov, CDC, ATSDR, ODPHP, MedlinePlus) are U.S. government guidance. The two journal papers and the ACH page do not state an audience in the parts quoted. Nothing here covers our Spanish (es-MX) pages; plain-language rules in Spanish need their own sources.
- **Long reference pages.** The CDC index was written for single public-health materials. Applying "one main message" to an 11-study evidence page is our inference.
- **Repetition that helps.** The only "repeat to reinforce" lead is an unread snippet.
- **Readability formulas.** One trial reported no evidence of a main effect of reading grade level on knowledge. None of the official pages quoted here gives a grade target.
